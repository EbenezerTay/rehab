About the study

What is PREFERD?

PREFERD stands for PRotEcting FEet at Risk in people with Diabetes. It is a research programme led by the Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health at the National University of Singapore.

What are researchers studying?

The study follows people with diabetes who may be at higher risk of foot complications. Researchers will examine factors associated with peripheral arterial disease, nerve damage and the development of new foot wounds.

What factors are considered?

The research looks beyond one aspect of health. It considers a range of factors that may help explain differences in foot health and risk.

Why this research matters

Small changes in foot health can have a big impact.

Diabetes can affect the nerves and blood vessels supplying the feet and legs. Reduced sensation or poor circulation may make it harder to notice injuries and can affect how well wounds heal.

Researchers want to understand why some people develop these complications and others do not. Better evidence may help identify people at risk earlier and inform more personalised prevention in the future.

Taking part

Could you be eligible?

The points below are a preliminary guide. The research team will confirm eligibility through the official screening process.

You may be eligible if you…

  • Have been diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes and are aged 21 years and above.
  • Have had diabetes for 10 years or more, OR have a diabetes-related complication: diabetic eye disease, kidney disease, blocked leg arteries, or nerve damage in the legs or feet.
  • Can communicate in English, Mandarin, Malay or Tamil.
  • Will be residing in Singapore for the next 3 years.
  • Are not pregnant.

You may not be able to join if you…

  • Have had a prior amputation.
  • Have an active foot ulcer.
  • A previous first foot ulcer that has fully healed may still be considered upon screening.
Please note: This is not a diagnosis or confirmation that you can join. Eligibility depends on the full inclusion and exclusion criteria and assessment by the authorised study team. If you are unsure, contact the team rather than ruling yourself out.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions.

Still have questions? Contact the research team and they can explain the study in more detail.

Is taking part compulsory?

No. Taking part is voluntary. You can take time to consider the information and ask questions before deciding. The approved consent form and research team will explain your rights, including withdrawal.

Will the assessments be painful?

The assessments are non-invasive and are not intended to be painful. They include checks of foot circulation and nerve sensation, ankle flexibility and strength measurements, as well as walking and other assessments. The research team will explain what each assessment involves before it begins. Ask the team if you have concerns about a particular procedure.

How long will my first visit take?

The initial screening takes approximately 30 minutes. If you are eligible, you can continue with the full baseline assessment on the same visit, which brings the total visit to approximately 75 minutes.

How much will I be reimbursed?

You receive $30 for the initial 30-minute screening visit. If you are eligible and complete the full 75-minute baseline assessment on the same visit, you receive an additional $30 ($60 in total). Annual follow-up visits over the 3 years are reimbursed at $70 (Year 1), $80 (Year 2) and $90 (Year 3).

What if I do not qualify?

If you do not meet the criteria after screening, you will not proceed to the main study assessment and no further visits are required. You will still receive the $30 reimbursement for your screening visit.

How long does the study last?

The study follows participants over three years, with an in-person visit once a year and interim follow-ups approximately every six months, according to the study protocol.

How will my information be protected?

The study follows institutional ethics oversight (NHG DSRB) and strict confidentiality. Personal and research information is handled under the study's approved procedures and applicable institutional requirements. The research team will provide the official privacy and data-use information during the consent process. Please do not send medical records or sensitive health details through this website.

Can I stop participating after I join?

Participation is voluntary. You may discuss withdrawal with the research team at any time. The team will explain how withdrawal works and what happens to information already collected, in line with the approved study documents.

Where will the assessments take place?

MD1 – Tahir Foundation Building, 12 Science Drive 2, Singapore 117549. It is a 10-minute walk from Kent Ridge MRT. Please confirm the exact appointment location and arrival instructions with the research team before travelling.
